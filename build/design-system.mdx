Phase 2 takes the design direction you approved at the end of Round 2 and locks it into a complete, precise design system. By the end of this phase, every color has an exact hex code, every font has a name, the page has a chosen archetype, and the section order is set. This system becomes the specification Claude follows throughout Phases 3–5 — no guessing, no improvising mid-build.

## What Phase 2 Produces

Claude presents the complete design system before a single line of code is written:

- **Color palette** — exact hex codes for primary, accent, background, surface, text, and muted tones
- **Font pairing** — Google Font names for your headline and body typefaces, chosen for your brand's vibe
- **Page archetype** — one of 8 proven structural templates, matched to your business type
- **Section order** — the exact sequence of sections the page will follow

If you want changes to any of these, Phase 2 is the right moment. Adjustments here take seconds. Changing the color system after Phase 4 means rewriting every component.

## How Claude Chooses Your Design Direction

Claude uses its built-in `ui-ux-pro-max` design intelligence — a curated database of 161 color palettes, 57 font pairings, and 50+ visual styles — to generate recommendations specific to your industry and vibe. It runs a search against your industry category and answers from Round 2, then selects the palette and pairing most likely to perform well for your audience.

If that tool is unavailable, Claude falls back to hand-curated industry palette tables and vibe-to-font mapping tables, then explains exactly what it chose and why: *"I went with a warm terracotta and off-white palette because it fits the artisan food space well — earthy, approachable, not trendy."*

<Tip>
  Claude always tells you what it chose and why. If the explanation doesn't match what you had in mind, that's the moment to say so — "actually I want something more modern" — and Claude will adjust.
</Tip>

## The 8 Page Archetypes

Claude selects the archetype that best fits your business type and goals. Each archetype defines the section order, CTA strategy, and component approach for the whole page.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="1. Hero-Centric" icon="star">
    **Best for:** Portfolios, creative agencies, product launches

    The hero IS the page — a full-viewport first impression with one bold CTA. Work or product grids follow, then a single large testimonial. Everything serves the opening moment.
  </Card>
  <Card title="2. Conversion-Optimized" icon="arrow-trend-up">
    **Best for:** SaaS, apps, subscription services, lead generation

    Every section pushes toward one action. Includes a problem/solution section, objection-handling FAQ, and the CTA appears after every major trust moment.
  </Card>
  <Card title="3. Feature-Rich" icon="grid">
    **Best for:** Tech products, multi-service agencies, platforms

    Comprehensive showcase with alternating text-left/image-right feature sections. Each capability gets space to breathe. Secondary CTAs after each feature detail.
  </Card>
  <Card title="4. Minimal" icon="minus">
    **Best for:** Luxury brands, personal brands, high-end services

    Maximum whitespace. Strong typography does the heavy lifting. Every element is intentional — no decorative filler, no generic grids, no urgency signals.
  </Card>
  <Card title="5. Social-Proof-Heavy" icon="users">
    **Best for:** Consultants, coaches, B2B services

    Trust-first structure. Client logos, a testimonials carousel, and a detailed case study all appear before you ask for any action. The CTA lands at peak credibility.
  </Card>
  <Card title="6. Interactive Demo" icon="play">
    **Best for:** SaaS products, developer tools, design tools

    "See it working" instead of "read about it." The hero contains an embedded demo or interactive preview. A "How it works" section reinforces what visitors just experienced.
  </Card>
  <Card title="7. Trust & Authority" icon="shield-check">
    **Best for:** Law firms, financial services, healthcare

    Credible, established, safe. Credentials strip, team highlights with full names, and a detailed footer with address, phone, and legal links. Formal CTA language throughout.
  </Card>
  <Card title="8. Storytelling" icon="book-open">
    **Best for:** Nonprofits, artisan businesses, food and beverage

    Narrative-driven scrolling experience. Origin story, impact section, then an emotional CTA after the story lands. Designed to build connection before asking for action.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Claude tells you which archetype it chose and why: *"Based on your coaching business, I'm using the Social-Proof-Heavy pattern — trust signals need to come before the ask for clients hiring you."* If you'd prefer a different archetype, say so and Claude switches.

## Design Principles Claude Follows

### No AI Slop Colors

Claude avoids the visual fingerprints of generic AI-generated sites:

- No cyan or teal on dark backgrounds
- No purple-to-blue gradients
- No neon accents on dark mode
- No pure black (`#000`) or pure white (`#fff`)

Instead, colors are chosen using OKLCH — a perceptually uniform color space that produces more natural, harmonious palettes than HSL. Neutrals are tinted toward your brand hue (even 2–3% creates subconscious cohesion), and dark mode is treated as a distinct design rather than just inverted light mode.

### No Banned Fonts

These fonts are blocked because they're the defaults every AI tool reaches for:

> Inter, Roboto, Arial, Open Sans, Helvetica, system-ui defaults

All fonts come from Google Fonts, loaded via `next/font/google` with `display: "swap"`. Choices are made based on your vibe — Playfair Display for elegance, Space Grotesk for modern/clean, Syne for bold/impactful, Fredoka for playful/warm.

### Asymmetric, Intentional Layouts

Claude doesn't center everything. Left-aligned text with asymmetric layouts feels more considered than a centered stack. Card grids with identical icon + heading + text layouts are avoided — sections are varied in structure, rhythm, and visual weight.

### The AI Slop Test

Before finalizing any design direction, Claude asks internally: *If someone looked at this and said "AI made this," would they be right?* If yes, the direction gets reconsidered. A distinctive interface should make someone ask "how was this made?" — not "which AI made this?"

## How to Iterate

Phase 2 is designed for iteration. If the design direction doesn't feel right, tell Claude specifically what's off:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Color Feedback" icon="palette">
    - "The colors feel too cold — I want something warmer"
    - "This looks too corporate for what I do"
    - "Can we go darker overall?"
    - "I want a bolder accent color"
  </Card>
  <Card title="Font Feedback" icon="type">
    - "The headline font feels too formal"
    - "I want something more modern, not so editorial"
    - "Can the body font be easier to read?"
    - "I like the headline but not the body"
  </Card>
  <Card title="Archetype Feedback" icon="layout">
    - "I'd rather lead with testimonials than features"
    - "Can we go more minimal?"
    - "I want it to feel like more of a story"
    - "We have a lot of features — give each one more room"
  </Card>
  <Card title="Vibe Feedback" icon="sparkles">
    - "It feels too safe — I want something bolder"
    - "I want it to feel more premium, less startup"
    - "This is too playful for my audience"
    - "I want it to feel handcrafted, not tech-y"
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Claude adjusts and re-presents the design system until you approve it. Once you do, Phase 3 begins immediately — no further prompting needed.

<Warning>
  Once you approve the design system and Phase 3 begins, changing the color palette or archetype requires going back to Phase 2. It's worth taking a moment here to make sure the direction feels right before Claude starts writing code.
</Warning>
